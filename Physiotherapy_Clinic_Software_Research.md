*COMPETITIVE PRODUCT RESEARCH  /  GLOBAL SHORTLIST*

# Physiotherapy clinic management software

*10 platforms, their real workflows, and the patterns worth learning*

Research date: 25 September 2026  |  138 linked official sources

**The main conclusion.** A clinic-management product is not just a booking calendar. The useful unit is a treatment episode: patient identity, planned visits, therapist availability, clinical work, money and communication must remain consistent when something changes. The examples in this report show different ways to solve that problem. [[NO08][NO08], [ZA11][ZA11], [HA10][HA10], [SP06][SP06]]

**The best references depend on the job.** Study Cliniko for a focused administration product; Jane for the patient relationship; Nookal, Zanda and splose for allied-health workflow detail; TM3 for referrals and linked booking; Halaxy for free-core economics and discovery. WebPT, Prompt and Raintree are especially useful references for insurance-heavy rehabilitation operations. These are research judgments, not measured rankings. [[CL14][CL14], [JA03][JA03], [NO03][NO03], [ZA14][ZA14], [SP01][SP01], [TM01][TM01], [HA01][HA01], [WE02][WE02], [PR02][PR02], [RA01][RA01]]

**Three details that change a product decision.** Cliniko documents patient cancellation but not direct patient rescheduling. Zanda and splose limit self-rescheduling to the same therapist, service and location. Prompt's own pages disagree about whether patients can reschedule online. A generic feature checklist would hide all three distinctions. [[CL09][CL09], [ZA05][ZA05], [SP05][SP05], [PR02][PR02], [PR03][PR03]]

**How to read the evidence.** Numbered source codes are clickable. Help guides support the documented workflows; product pages support advertised capabilities, not proven outcomes. Version, region, plan and roadmap qualifications are kept next to the relevant feature. A demo check marks an unresolved detail, not proof that a feature is absent. No authenticated account or real patient transaction was tested.

Start with the comparison on pages 2-4. The ten profiles follow. The final section explains the common patterns and what they imply for a new clinic product.

## Cliniko

*Simple clinic administration with low-friction patient booking*

**Where it fits.** A strong benchmark for a small or growing outpatient clinic that wants appointments, records and billing in one browser-based system. It sells the same core feature set across practitioner-count tiers, rather than reserving basic workflows for expensive plans. This makes it especially useful for studying a focused clinic-management product. [[CL01][CL01], [CL14][CL14]]

**Clinic setup and staff login.** The clinic configures locations, practitioners, appointment types and online availability. Each staff member uses a separate login; two-factor authentication can be required. The account-finder flow can email a discovery code, but that is not passwordless patient login: staff still sign into their clinic account with credentials. [[CL04][CL04], [CL05][CL05], [CL06][CL06]]

**New patient and booking journey.** The patient opens a hosted booking page or an embedded version on the clinic website, chooses the relevant location, service, practitioner and available time, then enters basic details. No patient account is required for this booking flow. The system can match an existing record or create a new one. The clinic can show practitioner photos, descriptions and booking terms. [[CL03][CL03], [CL04][CL04]]

**Forms and registration.** Booking is not the same as completing clinical registration. The clinic can send a secure intake form in a confirmation, reminder or follow-up. Connected form fields update selected patient-record fields; clinical information can have tighter visibility. This separates the short booking transaction from a longer medical-history questionnaire. [[CL11][CL11], [CL12][CL12]]

**Scheduling and recurring treatment.** Staff can create daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly repeats with a chosen interval and number of occurrences. Group appointments have capacity controls; fully booked groups are no longer available online. These are different scheduling objects from one-to-one visits. The public documentation also provides a staff-managed waitlist with patient preferences. [[CL08][CL08], [CL16][CL16], [CL13][CL13]]

**Rescheduling and cancellation.** Staff can move an appointment by dragging it or using Reschedule, then confirm the change. The current guide explicitly says patients cannot directly reschedule. Patients can cancel through a message link when the clinic enables it and the notice period allows it. Fully prepaid online bookings have a self-cancellation restriction; deposits are treated differently. [[CL09][CL09], [CL10][CL10]]

## Cliniko | operations and business

**Notifications.** Email and SMS can carry confirmations, reminders, form links and cancellation links. A staff reschedule can trigger an updated confirmation and practitioner notification. Do not confuse the waitlist with an automatic gap-filling engine: Cliniko documents staff filtering and acting on the list, not automatic offers to every matching patient. [[CL09][CL09], [CL11][CL11], [CL13][CL13]]

**Clinical work and money.** The product covers treatment notes, patient records, invoices, payments, reporting and telehealth. Its booking system can collect online payments through Stripe. A useful design lesson is that a booked visit, an invoice and a payment are related but different records; paid-booking rules can therefore limit what a patient is allowed to change online. [[CL03][CL03], [CL10][CL10], [CL14][CL14]]

**Website and app design.** The patient experience is a short booking form rather than a mandatory account dashboard. Staff work in the browser. The official mobile guide describes adding Cliniko to the phone home screen; that is a browser shortcut, not proof of a separately installed native application. Practitioner photos and service descriptions help patients make the correct booking choice. [[CL03][CL03], [CL04][CL04], [CL07][CL07]]

**Pricing and operating costs.** Published USD monthly tiers are $45 for one practitioner, $95 for 2-5, $145 for 6-8, $195 for 9-12, $295 for 13-25 and $395 for 26-200. Administrative users and locations are not separately metered in those tiers. SMS is $0.10 per message; third-party services can add charges. The trial is 30 days. [[CL01][CL01]]

**How it markets and reduces switching friction.** The public site emphasizes simplicity, human support, transparent pricing and educational resources. Managed migration is an important part of the offer. However, the self-service import tool is narrower: it is not a general importer for complete notes, appointment and invoice histories. A clinic should distinguish a managed transfer from uploading its own CSV files. [[CL02][CL02], [CL15][CL15]]

**Distinctive lesson and demo checks.** The lesson is restraint: make the everyday calendar, records and billing reliable without forcing every patient to create another password. Test a fully prepaid booking versus a deposit booking, cancellation near the cutoff, duplicate-patient matching, and the exact historical data included in migration. For a product requiring patient-led rescheduling, this is a meaningful limitation, not a hidden feature assumption. [[CL03][CL03], [CL09][CL09], [CL10][CL10], [CL15][CL15]]

## Jane

*Patient portal, mobile experience and clinic-growth tools*

**Where it fits.** Jane is a useful benchmark for clinics that care about the full patient experience: finding a service, booking, completing forms, paying and returning for care. Its physiotherapy offering connects clinical documentation with home-exercise support through Physitrack. Regional billing capabilities differ, so a clinic should evaluate its own country rather than assume every integration is global. [[JA02][JA02], [JA03][JA03]]

**Clinic setup and staff authentication.** Staff use individual profiles and clinic access permissions. Two-step verification adds a code to the password login, with authenticator and SMS options; phone-call verification has regional limits. Clinics can enforce verification across staff. Treatment definitions, practitioner shifts and online-booking settings determine what patients can actually book. [[JA12][JA12], [JA08][JA08], [JA09][JA09]]

**Patient registration and account structure.** Online booking or a clinic welcome email leads into the patient account. The portal stores upcoming and past visits, forms, invoices and other clinic-facing information. The patient mobile app has its own account which is linked to clinic accounts: the app password and an individual clinic password are not automatically the same. This is a significant multi-clinic identity decision. [[JA04][JA04], [JA06][JA06]]

**Booking on the mobile app.** The documented path is clinic, discipline or practitioner, treatment, available time and confirmation, with family-member selection when relevant. My Appointments separates upcoming and past visits and supports booking again. The native patient app is documented for Canada and the United States; that should not be presented as worldwide availability. [[JA05][JA05], [JA07][JA07]]

**Recurring appointments and the staff calendar.** Staff build a repeated treatment schedule and preview each occurrence against working shifts. The preview distinguishes available dates, conflicts and staff unavailability before the series is saved. Staff can also move or copy visits from the calendar. This is a better model to study than blindly generating the same slot every week. [[JA08][JA08], [JA09][JA09]]

**Patient changes and clinic control.** Patients can cancel or reschedule in their account when permitted. Once the clinic cutoff is reached, the action becomes a request to contact the clinic rather than an unrestricted change. The guide also describes a short grace period for a just-created booking. The clinic therefore retains control without making every routine change a receptionist task. [[JA04][JA04]]

## Jane | operations and business

**Reminders, waitlists and communication.** Waitlist storage and automatic waitlist notifications are not the same entitlement. The notification tools are limited to Thrive and eligible legacy plans. They match patients to openings and can temporarily hold an offered slot. Jane also documents secure portal messaging; a clinician starts the conversation and the patient can reply. Confirm the appropriate messaging channels for the clinic region. [[JA10][JA10], [JA11][JA11], [JA04][JA04]]

**Clinical and commercial workflow.** Intake forms, charting, invoices, payment collection, telehealth and optional insurance workflows sit around the appointment. Physitrack connects exercise prescription with clinical records. Jane also offers AI Scribe, websites, SEO and review tools. These should be treated as a product family with plan and add-on boundaries, not as one all-inclusive subscription. [[JA03][JA03], [JA01][JA01], [JA02][JA02]]

**Interface design worth studying.** The patient experience is account-centred: My Appointments, forms and financial history stay together. The mobile app adds a repeat-use surface, while the clinic calendar remains a work tool for staff. Family booking is handled as a deliberate selection, not by treating a shared email address as proof that two people are the same patient. [[JA04][JA04], [JA07][JA07]]

**Pricing snapshot.** US monthly starting prices are Balance $54, Practice $79 and Thrive $99. Balance is a small-volume solo plan with a 20-appointment monthly limit; additional practitioner charges apply in the larger plans. AI Scribe is advertised at $15 per opted-in clinician monthly. Insurance and website products add costs; Jane Websites is listed at $59 monthly. [[JA01][JA01]]

**How it markets itself and its customers.** Jane uses practitioner-oriented education, webinars, community, ambassadors and customer stories to sell the software. Separately, it sells tools that help a clinic market itself, including websites, SEO and reviews. Those are different acquisition loops: one brings clinics to Jane, the other brings patients to a clinic. [[JA02][JA02]]

**Discovery, limitations and demo checks.** Practitioner Search is documented as an early-access beta in British Columbia, not a mature global patient marketplace. Test the separation between app and clinic accounts, parent/child access, cancellation deadlines, waitlist-plan eligibility and local billing support. The most useful lesson is an integrated patient relationship, with explicit regional and commercial boundaries. [[JA13][JA13], [JA05][JA05], [JA06][JA06], [JA10][JA10]]

## Nookal

*Case-based allied-health workflows and configurable online booking*

**Where it fits.** Nookal is useful for studying clinics where a patient can have different cases, services, funders and appointment types. The product combines scheduling, notes, intake and billing rather than treating each visit as an isolated calendar entry. Its current tiers also separate basic operations from the richer Patient Portal. [[NO01][NO01], [NO03][NO03]]

**Clinic setup and staff access.** The clinic defines locations, services or classes, practitioners, working schedules and what is bookable online. Staff sign into Nookal separately from patients. Availability includes recurring working patterns, breaks and leave. The exact staff MFA enforcement and recovery policy should be demonstrated rather than inferred from the existence of a public login page. [[NO05][NO05], [NO11][NO11], [NO14][NO14]]

**Current booking journey.** The guide updated on 15 September 2026 describes the new flow, not only the legacy one. A patient starts with location or practitioner, chooses a service or class, then selects a date and time. They enter their details or use an existing Online Booking Code. That code is distinct from a normal portal password. [[NO04][NO04]]

**Payment and new registration.** If payment is required, the patient must complete the configured payment step; otherwise the flow can allow booking without immediate payment. Successful bookings produce a confirmation. The clinic can make services, classes and practitioners selectively visible, so online booking is a controlled view of the diary rather than exposure of every empty staff slot. [[NO04][NO04], [NO05][NO05]]

**Optional patient portal login.** The Patient Portal is available on Professional and Enterprise. The invitation path lets a patient establish a password, with multi-factor verification through email or SMS. The clinic can add portal-access links to communications. Thus Nookal has two related surfaces: the booking flow using details or a booking code, and a fuller authenticated portal. [[NO06][NO06], [NO07][NO07]]

**Recurring schedules and changes.** A staff appointment links the patient to a case and service. Recurring booking requires a date check before saving. Staff can move appointments, update status, cancel with a reason, or cancel and rebook. Recurring-booking tools also manage a series. Patients can cancel via the confirmation link subject to the location rules. Detailed self-rescheduling conditions remain less clearly documented than staff changes. [[NO08][NO08], [NO09][NO09], [NO10][NO10], [NO04][NO04]]

## Nookal | operations and business

**Confirmation and reminder system.** Confirmation campaigns can be scoped by service or class, location and practitioner, and can include form links. Communication consent and SMS credits matter. Staff should avoid overlapping campaigns that send duplicates. The online-booking flow also supports a clinic message-centre event and optional practitioner notifications, separate from the patient confirmation. [[NO12][NO12], [NO04][NO04]]

**Clinical, financial and operational work.** The product offers patient records, clinical notes, invoicing, payments, intake forms and reporting. Case-linked appointments are particularly useful when the same patient has more than one treatment episode. Professional adds capabilities beyond Essentials, while AI and voice functions may require additional paid options. Availability of country-specific claims integrations must be checked regionally. [[NO01][NO01], [NO03][NO03], [NO08][NO08]]

**Website and application design.** The public booking flow is configurable rather than fixed: location-first and practitioner-first routes are both documented. The staff help material distinguishes Classic and Fresh interfaces, so training screenshots may not match every account. The clearer design lesson is to keep service, case, practitioner and location visible at booking time, reducing ambiguity later in billing. [[NO04][NO04], [NO08][NO08]]

**Pricing snapshot.** The pricing page lists Essentials at USD $65 and Professional at USD $89 per practitioner monthly. It also lists different regional prices, including AUD $49/$59 and GBP 29/39; these are regional tariffs, not exchange-rate conversions. Enterprise is a separate offering. Patient Portal access is not an Essentials feature. Confirm SMS, AI and implementation costs in the actual quote. [[NO01][NO01], [NO06][NO06]]

**How it markets and positions the business.** Nookal uses profession-specific pages, demonstrations and educational material to position itself as an allied-health operations system. Its website highlights integrated workflows and newer AI assistance. The sensible interpretation is reduced administrative switching between tasks, not proof that every clinic will achieve a stated time-saving or clinical outcome. [[NO02][NO02], [NO03][NO03]]

**Important boundaries and demo checks.** The physical-therapy marketing page advertises simple patient changes, but the inspected help material gives much firmer detail for cancellation than for self-rescheduling. Ask for a live portal demonstration, including a paid recurring visit. Also check export scope and account closure: the cancellation guide requires owner contact and a completed checklist, with processing time before billing stops. [[NO03][NO03], [NO13][NO13]]

## Zanda

*Detailed self-service rules, recurring visits and session-pack accounting*

**Where it fits.** Zanda is the renamed Power Diary, not an additional competitor to count separately. It is a useful reference for allied-health clinics needing a calendar, client portal, notes, forms, invoicing and structured packages. Its documentation is unusually explicit about restrictions and financial side effects, making it valuable for product design research. [[ZA02][ZA02], [ZA04][ZA04], [ZA12][ZA12], [ZA14][ZA14]]

**Staff versus patient login.** Staff use an email-and-password login with optional two-factor authentication that can be required for the team. Patient portal registration uses an email username and password, followed by verification. SMS verification requires a mobile number; email verification is another option. This is not a phone-number-only, passwordless login model. [[ZA16][ZA16], [ZA13][ZA13], [ZA03][ZA03]]

**Registration and booking.** The clinic controls whether new clients can register and whether people can browse before logging in. Registration can match an existing client instead of creating another record. The portal exposes the services or classes made available by the clinic, along with appointments, forms, files and payment-related information. Dependants have a documented portal workflow. [[ZA03][ZA03], [ZA04][ZA04]]

**Recurring appointments.** The staff system supports changes to one occurrence, that occurrence and following ones, or the entire recurring set. Changes to a series can rebuild future appointments, so existing invoices and paid visits create important restrictions. A safe workflow must explain the edit scope before saving, rather than silently altering every future session. [[ZA06][ZA06]]

**Patient self-rescheduling.** The portal permits eligible future individual appointments to move to a different date or time. The practitioner, service and location stay the same; it is not an unrestricted switch to another therapist. Group visits, booking settings, notice periods and appointment state affect eligibility. A session-pack booking also needs the relevant linked service to be valid. [[ZA05][ZA05]]

**Cancellation and refunds.** A portal cancellation does not automatically refund a paid appointment. Paid invoices remain and a refund must be handled separately; unpaid invoice handling is different. Cancellation by SMS has another caveat: it does not automatically delete the invoice. Therefore a clinic must test the portal and SMS routes independently, even though both appear to cancel the visit. [[ZA07][ZA07], [ZA09][ZA09]]

## Zanda | operations and business

**Notification behaviour.** Zanda supports email and SMS reminders, confirmations and reply processing. Keyword replies can update confirmation or cancellation status. Portal registration, booking, rescheduling and cancellation have configurable client and practitioner notifications. Overlapping global reminders and automation rules can create duplicates, so the configuration is part of the product workflow, not merely a marketing checkbox. [[ZA08][ZA08], [ZA10][ZA10]]

**Packages: the most important accounting detail.** Session packs can track sessions, time or money and can carry expiry or referral-related limits. A unit is consumed when allocated to an invoice, not simply when attendance is marked. This is a different model from deducting a session only after treatment. It affects cancellations, future reservations, remaining-session displays and staff explanations to patients. [[ZA11][ZA11], [ZA12][ZA12]]

**Clinical work and operations.** Practitioners work from the calendar into client information, notes, forms and files. The documented training includes a Physitrack integration. The wider setup also includes a customizable practice-policy manual and operational tools. These features are relevant to clinic consistency, but a policy template is not a substitute for a clinic reviewing its own procedures. [[ZA14][ZA14], [ZA15][ZA15]]

**Interface design.** There are two clear workspaces: a staff calendar for managing the practice, and a client portal for personal actions. Restricting patient rescheduling to the same service, location and practitioner keeps the portal simpler while avoiding a hidden change in price or treatment responsibility. The trade-off is that therapist substitution still needs clinic involvement. [[ZA04][ZA04], [ZA05][ZA05]]

**Business model and marketing.** Standard USD pricing starts at $19 monthly for Starter, with its listed restrictions, and $49 for Growth before additional-practitioner charges. Growth adds $19 per additional practitioner; administrative users are not the same billing unit. The site uses a trial, migration support, introductory promotions and a guarantee to lower adoption friction. SMS and other optional services need a separate cost check. [[ZA01][ZA01]]

**Best lesson and demo checks.** Study the rules behind the interface, not only the calendar. Demonstrate one changed occurrence in a paid recurring series; cancellation through the portal versus SMS; an expired pack; and restoring a wrongly allocated session. For a new product, write the package ledger and refund policy before deciding what the patient sees as remaining sessions. [[ZA05][ZA05], [ZA06][ZA06], [ZA07][ZA07], [ZA09][ZA09], [ZA11][ZA11]]

## TM3

*Physiotherapy workflows, referral networks and a newly revised booking journey*

**Where it fits.** TM3 belongs to the Blue Zinc product family and is especially useful for studying physiotherapy workflows involving referrals, insurers and structured patient records. Pronto is a related referral-network product, not simply another name for the TM3 diary. Evaluate the combined workflow and its commercial modules rather than assuming everything is part of the base subscription. [[TM01][TM01], [TM10][TM10], [TM11][TM11]]

**Setup and identity.** Connect is the patient-facing booking surface. Clinics configure branding, registration permission, online booking limits and provisional approval. Staff have separate account access and password-management help. New Connect documentation shows a magic-link screen in the patient flow; older online-booking guides use an account/password pattern. Do not treat those older screenshots as proof of the current authentication sequence. [[TM02][TM02], [TM04][TM04], [TM16][TM16]]

**New online booking design.** The 2026 flow guides patients through the steps relevant to the chosen service. It can show available dates, practitioner and location choices, embedded registration forms and checkout. The guided selector can help distinguish an assessment from follow-up, private payment from insurance, or a referral pathway. The exact steps depend on modules and service configuration. [[TM03][TM03], [TM04][TM04]]

**Linked appointments and recurring availability.** Companion booking can require two linked appointments with ordering or timing constraints. *04  /  PRACTICAL EVALUATION*

## A demo that reveals the real system

Use the same fictional clinic and patient in every demo. The following are proposed tests, not claims that all ten vendors pass them. Ask the demonstrator to show both the patient screen and the staff record after each action.

| **Test** | **What a convincing demonstration must show** |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Register without duplication | Create a patient at reception, then book online with the same details. Show matching, a changed phone number, a parent booking for a child and access to the correct record. |
| 2. Build a treatment series | Book eight one-hour weekly sessions. Put a holiday, another-location booking and a therapist absence in the middle. Show conflict preview before saving. |
| 3. Change one visit only | Move visit four to another time or therapist. Show patient approval if required, unchanged future visits, the audit trail and replacement reminders. |
| 4. Cancel a paid visit | Compare before-cutoff, late cancellation and no-show. Show the appointment state, invoice, refund, package credit and patient message separately. |
| 5. Exhaust a package or authorization | Use the final session, then attempt another booking. Show whether future bookings reserve units and what happens when a cancellation releases one. |
| 6. Recover an opening safely | Have two patients accept one offered slot. Show how overbooking is prevented, how the losing patient is informed and when the offer expires. |
| 7. Break communication | Use missing consent, an invalid phone, a failed SMS and a duplicated event. Show delivery status, staff follow-up and prevention of duplicate messages. |
| 8. Export and leave | Export a sample including patients, notes, attachments, appointments and financial history. Check readability and access during the final migration period. |

**Security and integration questions.** Request a permission matrix for admin, therapist and patient; MFA and recovery rules; access logs; hosting and backup details; incident procedures; data ownership and retention terms; and documented integration/export capabilities. Do not treat a vendor's general compliance statement as proof that the clinic's configuration is appropriate.

**What to record.** For each test, capture available / configuration needed / paid add-on / manual workaround / not supported / unresolved. Record the version, region and plan. A manual workflow can be acceptable; an undisclosed manual workflow is the larger risk.

*06  /  SYNTHESIS*

## Common patterns | how the systems work

The conclusions below are analysis of the documented examples, not a claim that every platform implements the same internal architecture.

**1. The clinic record comes before the app account.** A patient can already exist because reception or a referral created the record. Booking, a portal invitation and mobile-app sign-up are later access paths. Cliniko's guest flow, Jane's linked app accounts and Halaxy's invitations show different solutions. A new system should keep login identity, patient profile, family access and clinical record separate. [[CL03][CL03], [JA06][JA06], [HA05][HA05]]

**2. Availability is a set of rules, not empty calendar space.** The real question is whether a suitable service can happen with the right practitioner, duration, location and resources. Insurance-heavy products add authorization constraints. Nookal's case/service booking, WebPT's resource rules and splose's cross-location blocking illustrate why a simple free/busy check is insufficient. [[NO08][NO08], [WE02][WE02], [SP04][SP04]]

**3. A treatment series needs individual exceptions.** Physiotherapy often involves a planned series, but staff leave and patient changes affect individual dates. Jane previews conflicts, Zanda asks which part of a series to edit, and Nookal checks recurring dates. The product lesson is to preserve the overall plan while allowing a controlled exception, with a visible audit trail. [[JA08][JA08], [ZA06][ZA06], [NO08][NO08]]

**4. Cancellation is not one database update.** A visit can be cancelled while its invoice, payment or package allocation still needs attention. Halaxy makes status affect financial handling; Zanda explains invoice-based package consumption; splose documents manual refund work. Model the appointment status, financial ledger and session entitlement separately, and show their combined result to staff. [[HA10][HA10], [ZA11][ZA11], [SP05][SP05]]

**5. Patient freedom is intentionally limited.** Self-service rarely means unrestricted editing. The same-therapist/service/location rule in Zanda and splose protects the treatment and price context. Cliniko instead routes rescheduling through staff. Either design can work: the important part is an obvious explanation and a reliable way to contact the clinic when the online action is unavailable. [[ZA05][ZA05], [SP05][SP05], [CL09][CL09]]

**6. Communication is an event workflow.** Booking, changing, cancelling, arriving, missing a visit and leaving a balance are different events. Recipient preferences, timing and delivery status matter. Zanda's SMS/invoice caveat and splose's duplicate-accounting-reminder warning show that sending a message is not the same as correctly processing the underlying event. [[ZA09][ZA09], [SP10][SP10], [SP11][SP11]]

**7. The staff and patient interfaces should not look identical.** Patients need a small set of clear actions. Staff need context, exceptions, work queues and financial consequences. Prompt's task links and operational dashboards, and Halaxy's calendar-led workspace, are two examples of that split. The useful design target is simplicity for each role, not one identical screen for everyone. [[PR04][PR04], [PR02][PR02], [HA12][HA12]]

*06  /  SYNTHESIS*

## Common patterns | how the businesses compete

**8. There is more than one market.** The clinic-management problem changes with the payer. A small self-pay clinic and a large US rehabilitation group may both need a calendar, but authorization, claims and denial workflows substantially change the product. WebPT and Raintree make those differences clear. A new business should choose its first clinic type and region before copying an enterprise feature list. [[WE02][WE02], [WE07][WE07], [RA05][RA05]]

**9. Selling software and finding patients are different jobs.** Vendors use education, demos, communities and customer stories to win clinics. Some also sell tools that help clinics win patients: Jane offers website/SEO/review products, Prompt Plus manages leads and follow-up, and Halaxy provides a directory. These are related but separate businesses; a public clinic profile does not establish a functioning marketplace. [[JA02][JA02], [PR07][PR07], [HA01][HA01]]

**10. Lowering adoption risk is part of the product.** Transparent pricing, a trial and migration help reduce the effort of trying new software. Cliniko emphasizes managed transfers; Zanda uses trial and guarantee positioning. But switching out is equally important: TM3's extraction process affects ongoing access. A new vendor can build trust by making both onboarding and departure predictable. [[CL01][CL01], [CL15][CL15], [ZA01][ZA01], [TM15][TM15]]

**11. Monetization follows cost and workflow depth.** The sample contains practitioner-count pricing, plan-based feature gates, paid modules and a free-core/paid-usage model. Messaging, payments, claims and AI have separate economic costs. Cliniko, Nookal, Prompt and Halaxy illustrate these approaches. Price comparison should therefore use a complete clinic scenario, not only the cheapest advertised monthly amount. [[CL01][CL01], [NO01][NO01], [PR07][PR07], [HA02][HA02]]

**12. Integration matters more than feature count.** A useful integration carries the right patient, appointment and financial context through the workflow. Exercise prescription, insurer billing, accounting reminders and data migration each have different requirements. Jane's exercise integration, TM3's insurer setup and splose's duplicate-reminder warning show why a long integrations list alone does not prove a smooth experience. [[JA03][JA03], [TM10][TM10], [SP11][SP11]]

**13. AI is becoming a layer, not a replacement for sound operations.** Prompt and Raintree promote AI across documentation and administration. Some capabilities are current products; others are announcements or future rollout. The underlying appointment, permission and financial rules still need to be correct. Evaluate what the AI may read, suggest and execute, and which actions require a human decision. [[PR09][PR09], [RA07][RA07], [RA08][RA08]]

**Measure the business outcome.** For a new product, track activation of clinics, booking completion, no-shows, administrative time per visit, completed follow-ups, money collected and renewal/retention. These are proposed measures, not results established by this research. Measure change against the clinic's own starting point; avoid promising savings based only on vendor testimonials.

*06  /  RECOMMENDED BUILD ORDER*

## Common patterns | implications for your product

This section is a product recommendation, not a claim about competitor implementation. For a clinic-first app with admin, therapist and patient roles, the strongest opportunity is to make recurring treatment and its exceptions dependable before adding a marketplace or extensive automation.

**First: make one clinic run reliably.** Start with patient records, role-based access, the therapist calendar, services and working hours, recurring one-hour treatment plans, attendance, basic notes and a clear payment/session history. Make every cancellation or adjustment record who changed it and why. A therapist should see the right clinical information; an administrator should see the operational work needed to keep the day running.

**Keep the first change workflow controlled.** Admin-led rescheduling is a reasonable first version. Allow an administrator to change one visit without unintentionally shifting the remaining series. If patient approval is needed, represent a proposed replacement separately from a confirmed booking, keep the original status clear and send the final confirmation only after acceptance. Avoid silently assigning a different therapist.

**Use phone OTP as access, not as the patient record.** A phone-code login may suit your users, but one family can share a number and a patient can change numbers. Keep a durable patient identifier independent of the login method. Add explicit dependent access and a safe recovery process. The number used to receive a code should not itself determine permission to view every linked clinical record.

**Decide the session ledger before drawing the balance screen.** Define available, reserved, completed, cancelled-with-credit, cancelled-with-fee and manually adjusted sessions. Decide whether a future appointment reserves a unit and whether a no-show consumes it. Show a readable history explaining each balance change. Do not silently treat a cancelled appointment as a refund or assume a refunded payment always restores a treatment session.

**Then add carefully bounded patient self-service.** After the core is stable, add patient booking, forms, confirmations and reminders. Self-rescheduling can begin with a date/time change that keeps the therapist, service and location fixed. Present the notice rule before confirmation and give an obvious contact route when an action is blocked. A patient should never need to guess whether a request is still awaiting approval.

**Delay the expensive second business.** A waitlist engine, automated therapist substitution, insurance-heavy billing, AI agents and multi-clinic discovery can follow demonstrated demand. A marketplace requires useful supply, patient trust and acquisition, not merely a search screen. For an initial clinic pilot, better recurring-plan reliability and less receptionist rework are clearer goals than matching all ten platforms feature for feature.

**The strongest overall pattern.** The durable product is the system that keeps promises consistent: the patient knows when and with whom treatment will happen; the therapist knows what work is expected; the clinic knows what is owed; and every change reaches the right person. A polished booking page helps, but those connected rules are the core business.
